Under the hood

How it works

A two-person chat with no chat server. This page explains what that means, and where it stops being true.

In short

This site is one static page. It has no backend, no accounts and no database. When two people open it and type the same phrase, their browsers find each other and open a direct, encrypted connection. Messages travel over that connection and nowhere else.

Step by step

  1. You type a nickname and a phrase. The phrase is your room's password. Nobody checks it against a list, it only has to match.
  2. Your browser hashes the phrase. It computes SHA-256 of the lowercased phrase and keeps the first 32 hex characters. That is the room ID. The phrase itself is never sent anywhere.
  3. Browsers find each other. Using the open-source library Trystero, each browser posts a small signaling note to public Nostr relays, tagged with the room ID. The note is encrypted with your phrase, so relays only see unreadable data.
  4. A direct connection opens. When two browsers read each other's notes they set up a WebRTC connection. WebRTC data channels are encrypted in transit (DTLS).
  5. Messages and files go browser to browser. Nothing is sent to the site's host or to the relays. Nothing is written to disk or to a database.
  6. You close the tab and it's gone. There is no history to recover.

Photos and files

You can send a photo, a PDF or any other file, up to 50 MB. It travels over the same direct connection as messages, so no server sees it or keeps a copy.

  • The receiver decides. Files up to 10 MB arrive automatically. Bigger ones wait until the other person presses Accept, and nothing is transferred before that.
  • Only real pictures are shown inline. The app checks the file's first bytes, not its name or claimed type, and only displays PNG, JPEG, GIF and WebP. Everything else (PDF, documents, archives, even a file renamed to .png) is download-only and never opened automatically.
  • Treat files like email attachments. Anyone who knows the phrase can send you a file. Only open files from people you trust.
  • Files live in memory. They disappear when the tab closes, a very large file can make a phone struggle, and an interrupted transfer starts again from the beginning.

Known limits

  • The phrase is the only lock. Anyone who guesses it can join with any nickname. Use something long and random, such as three unrelated words, and share it in person or over a channel you trust. Avoid "test" or "chat".
  • No identity check. A nickname is just text. Nothing proves who is behind it.
  • Your IP is visible to the other person. That is how direct connections work. If that matters, this tool is not the right one.
  • Both people must be online at the same time. Messages can't wait for someone who isn't there.
  • Some networks block it. There is no TURN relay, so strict firewalls, some mobile networks and some corporate Wi-Fi can stop the connection. Try another network.
  • Built for small rooms. Every person connects to every other person, so it works best with two to a handful of people.
  • It trusts the code you load. If the host or the CDN were compromised, the page could be altered. That is why the source is public and small.

Not for situations where your safety depends on anonymity. This tool keeps messages off servers, not you off the network.
